SILVER BULL NEWSWIRE — THURSDAY, AUGUST 13

This is the Silver Bull Newswire on W3BC. Here are the stories setting the table for today’s show.

We begin with regulation, where the U.S. market-structure fight is getting more specific.

The Senate CLARITY Act vote remains pushed into September, but expectations are building around an SEC vote on August fourteenth involving a proposed Regulation Crypto framework.

Reporting in this morning’s file describes a potential path for certain issuers to raise up to seventy-five million dollars outside traditional registration while still providing audited financial statements and semiannual reports.

Fidelity is also seeking SEC approval to add staking capabilities to its Ethereum ETF, which is reported to manage more than eight hundred ninety-eight million dollars in assets.

That creates a bigger question for the industry: is the next phase of adoption really about more products, or is it about finally creating a market structure institutions can actually use?

Now to the macro picture.

United States CPI inflation slowed to three-point-four percent as expected. Bitcoin held near sixty-four thousand dollars while Treasury yields declined.

The price action is still messy, but the institutional adoption story continues moving underneath it.

Coinbase Institutional leadership is describing what it calls a second phase of institutional crypto adoption — one that goes beyond simply giving institutions access to Bitcoin and Ethereum.

BlackRock’s IBIT product has also lowered its swap minimum to one million dollars, reducing friction for Bitcoin holders looking to move BTC into the ETF structure without first selling it.

And TRON continues posting massive stablecoin activity. Reporting tied to Messari and Nansen says the network powered roughly two-point-one trillion dollars in USDT transfers during the second quarter.

That is real blockchain usage at scale, whether or not it shows up in the daily token-price conversation.

Now to the biggest institutional-market story on the board today.

Bloomberg reporting says JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Invesco and Citadel Securities put Wall Street’s blockchain experiment to a much bigger test.

For four hours in July, major financial firms reportedly traded stocks and Treasuries over blockchain networks.

That matters because this is no longer just about issuing a digital version of an asset and showing that the technology works.

The question is whether blockchain rails can become part of ordinary capital-market activity.

Bullish is also moving further into tokenized equities. The company says market participants executed trades of its tokenized shares on Bullish Exchange.

Crypto dot com is expanding tokenized stock derivatives as well, offering products that track approximately fifteen hundred United States stocks and ETFs, including names like Apple, Tesla and Nvidia.

These products provide price exposure, although they do not necessarily come with the same ownership rights as traditional shares.

Tokenization continues being pitched around faster settlement, lower trading costs, fractional access and twenty-four-seven markets.

But there is another side to that story.

One argument in this morning’s file says tokenization may not kill the banks at all. It may simply give large financial institutions new ways to control custody, compliance, liquidity and distribution.

So instead of eliminating intermediaries, better blockchain rails could actually help incumbents build new financial empires.

The trillion-dollar forecasts remain in the conversation too. Coverage again cites Citi’s estimate that tokenized securities could grow to roughly five-point-five trillion dollars by twenty thirty, including around two-point-six trillion dollars in tokenized equities.

Now to stablecoins, CBDCs and on-chain finance.

The Bank of England is testing Polygon alongside a simulated central bank digital currency in a dual-rail payment experiment aimed at small and midsize enterprise trade flows.

That is a useful example of how public blockchain infrastructure and central-bank money may eventually operate beside each other rather than in completely separate systems.

In the United States, the stablecoin debate remains tied to banking competition.

Senator Cynthia Lummis is rejecting claims that stablecoins are causing widespread deposit flight from community banks as the CLARITY Act fight continues.

At the same time, the industry is still debating which form of digital money ultimately wins: stablecoins, tokenized bank deposits or central bank digital currencies.

And Andre Cronje is arguing that the term DeFi may no longer describe what the industry has become.

His view is that the market is evolving into something broader: on-chain finance, or open finance.

That description may fit better as institutional funds, tokenized securities, stablecoins and traditional financial products begin using the same blockchain rails.

Ondo Finance is also pushing that market-structure conversation forward. The company filed a comment with the SEC around Regulation NMS Rule six-eleven, arguing for a market that better connects on-chain and off-chain securities.

The larger picture this morning is pretty clear.

Regulators are trying to build a usable framework. Bitcoin is holding near sixty-four thousand dollars as inflation cools. Institutional adoption is moving into its next phase. Wall Street firms are testing real stocks and Treasuries over blockchain networks. Tokenized equity products are expanding. And stablecoins, bank deposits, CBDCs and tokenized assets are beginning to collide inside the same financial system.
