WALL STREET KEEPS MOVING IN

First up: institutional adoption.

And at this point, I think we can officially retire the phrase “institutions are coming.” They’re here.

One report in this morning’s news argues that digital assets are entering a more mature institutional phase, with pension funds potentially becoming an important source of adoption as larger investors move toward more disciplined, strategic allocations.

Meanwhile, Goldman Sachs is making moves of its own. One of this morning’s reports highlights Goldman’s acquisition of NEOS, an ETF platform associated with yield-generating strategies.

And UBS continues building exposure to Bitcoin through BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust, with another report putting its disclosed position at nearly 90 million dollars as of June 30th.

Morgan Stanley is in the headlines too. Its Bitcoin ETF reportedly attracted roughly 371 million dollars in gross share contributions during its operating period.

So while somebody on your timeline is still explaining why crypto is going to zero... Wall Street apparently forgot to get the memo.

ETHEREUM ETF STAKING

Now here’s one worth watching.

A report this morning says Fidelity is seeking SEC approval to add staking and quarterly payouts to its Ethereum ETF, FETH.

That matters. Because the next evolution of crypto ETFs isn’t necessarily just: buy the asset and hold it. It could increasingly become: buy the asset, hold it, and generate yield.

If staking continues making its way into regulated investment products, that creates an entirely different conversation around Ethereum as an institutional asset.

THE SEC HITS THE BRAKES

But Washington isn’t exactly moving in a straight line.

The SEC abruptly canceled a meeting scheduled for today that was expected to address new crypto-related rules. Reuters reports the meeting was supposed to consider whether the agency should propose new crypto regulations.

Other reports in this morning’s digest say the agency was also expected to address initiatives involving digital assets and potentially blockchain-based trading.

And there’s another wrinkle. The SEC’s anticipated “innovation exemption” for tokenization is reportedly facing another delay amid concerns from Wall Street and the White House.

So if you’re keeping score at home: crypto regulation is moving forward... except when it moves sideways... or gets postponed... or somebody schedules a meeting and then cancels the meeting.

Government efficiency, baby.

TOKENIZED STOCKS ARE GETTING REAL

Now this is where things get interesting.

Tokenized stocks are becoming a significantly larger piece of the real-world-asset market.

One report in today’s rundown says tokenized stocks have gone from roughly 1.4 percent to more than 15 percent of total RWA market cap in a year, reaching around 2.5 billion dollars in distributed value.

That’s not some theoretical white paper anymore. That’s actual capital moving on-chain.

Another report says Uniswap’s RWA volume has reached a record 2.5 billion dollars.

And Bullish is pushing further into tokenization as well, with CEO Tom Farley highlighting tokenized shares trading on the company’s regulated infrastructure.

The bigger picture here is pretty simple: stocks trading twenty-four-seven, fractional ownership, blockchain settlement, global accessibility.

Traditional finance is slowly starting to look a whole lot more like crypto. Funny how that works.

SOLANA AND STELLAR PUSH INTO RWAs

And this isn’t happening on just one chain.

Today’s alerts put Solana’s RWA ecosystem around 3.9 billion dollars, along with growing holder counts.

Stellar is showing some serious movement too. One report says Stellar doubled its tokenized real-world assets during the second quarter to approximately 3.05 billion dollars, while also recording 11.4 billion dollars in stablecoin transfers during the quarter.

That is exactly why we keep talking about infrastructure on this show.

The next phase of crypto isn’t only memecoins going twenty-X overnight. It’s assets. Payments. Settlement. Ownership. Markets. And increasingly, putting real-world financial activity onto blockchain rails.

BITCOIN HAS A FIGHT ON ITS HANDS

Now let’s check on Grandpa Bitcoin.

One report says approximately 1.79 million Bitcoin are creating a potential wall of supply that could make upside breakouts more difficult.

Bitcoin miners are adding pressure too. Another story says miners have sold around 28,000 Bitcoin this year, representing roughly 1.78 billion dollars of selling pressure.

At the same time, institutional exposure continues growing.

Which gives us a pretty interesting tug-of-war: miners selling, existing supply overhead, institutions accumulating exposure, ETF money moving around, and Bitcoin sitting in the middle saying: “Y’all finished?”

Probably not.

CBDCs AREN’T DEAD EITHER

Meanwhile, the global CBDC debate continues.

India is expanding its use of a programmable central bank digital currency for welfare distribution, including a CBDC-based direct-benefit-transfer program.

Back in the United States, we’re basically having the opposite conversation. Lawmakers continue debating proposals designed to prevent the Federal Reserve from issuing a retail CBDC.

Some governments are asking: “How can we use CBDCs?” The United States is asking: “Should we even allow one?”

That battle isn’t going away anytime soon.
