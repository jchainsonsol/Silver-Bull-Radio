SILVER BULL NEWSWIRE — WEDNESDAY, AUGUST 12

This is the Silver Bull Newswire on W3BC. Here are the stories setting the table for today’s show.

We begin in Washington, where Congress is still working through crypto market structure, but regulators appear ready to move on their own timelines.

Reporting this morning says the SEC and CFTC are preparing new crypto-focused initiatives while the broader Senate effort remains stalled.

The SEC is also expected to consider a tailored offering regime for certain crypto-related investment contracts at an August fifteenth open meeting. The bigger question is whether workable agency rules can move faster than comprehensive legislation.

Outside the United States, BRICS nations are discussing potential links between their fast-payment systems and central bank digital currencies. That creates a very different digital-money path from the United States, where private stablecoins continue gaining importance.

Now to Bitcoin.

Bitcoin remains stuck in a narrow range, with reporting putting the market roughly between sixty-two and sixty-six thousand dollars. ETF demand is helping support the market, but selling from miners and other holders continues to offset some of those inflows.

United States spot Bitcoin ETFs brought in about eight hundred fifty-four million dollars last week, their strongest weekly haul since April. Wintermute says that is encouraging, but it wants to see sustained flows before turning fully bullish.

The institutional story extends beyond ETFs. Wintermute says institutions accounted for seventy-two percent of its spot over-the-counter volume during the first half of twenty twenty-six. That points to deeper professional liquidity even while Bitcoin itself remains range-bound.

Japanese Bitcoin treasury company Metaplanet also moved three thousand eight hundred eighty-one Bitcoin between wallets it controls. Blockchain data indicated the transfer was internal rather than a sale, another reminder that large on-chain movements do not automatically mean coins are headed to market.

Now to tokenized equities.

Tokenized stocks have reportedly reached about two-point-four-nine billion dollars in value, up roughly six hundred percent over the past year.

Crypto.com is rolling out tokenized stock derivatives as exchanges continue pushing into equity exposure. These products track share prices, although they do not necessarily provide the same ownership and shareholder rights as traditional stock.

Settlement is also becoming part of the story. Tokenized equity systems are being promoted as a way to shrink settlement from a full business day toward seconds.

On Robinhood Chain, decentralized liquidity is already forming around tokenized stocks, with Uniswap reported to hold the overwhelming majority of that liquidity.

But there is also an important reality check. A GSR executive says tokenization hype has outpaced actual usage on some heavily restricted, walled-garden platforms. The point is simple: launching a tokenized product is not the same thing as creating real liquidity or real market activity.

BlackRock is also continuing to expand tokenized money-market products as major financial institutions build out blockchain-based cash and settlement infrastructure.

Turning to real-world assets and payments, the broader RWA market is increasingly being discussed through the lens of collateral rather than simple token representation.

When stablecoins are included, reporting puts the wider tokenized real-world-asset landscape above seven hundred thirty billion dollars. The next step is making those assets productive inside lending, collateral and settlement systems.

Stablecoin card spending has reportedly moved above seven hundred fifty million dollars. In many cases, the stablecoin is converted into local currency at the point of sale, meaning consumers can use digital dollars while merchants continue receiving familiar money through existing card rails.

Payoneer leadership says that last-mile conversion into usable local currency may be where the stablecoin race is actually won.

Institutional Ethereum is also becoming more productive. Galaxy and Sharplink are launching a one-hundred-twenty-five-million-dollar fund designed to deploy institutional ETH into decentralized finance and other on-chain opportunities.

Tokenized Treasury products continue expanding as well, with Franklin Templeton highlighted as a major participant in tokenized United States Treasury bills.

And one more longer-term theme: Grayscale argues that AI agents may create new demand for blockchain infrastructure because autonomous systems need verifiable records, identity and payments.

The larger picture this morning is pretty clear.

Congress is moving slowly, regulators are moving anyway, institutional crypto liquidity is deepening, tokenized stocks are growing fast, real-world assets are becoming collateral, and stablecoins are moving further into everyday payments.
